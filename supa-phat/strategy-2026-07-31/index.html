04 Empire #14 · Sat 5 Sep, Perth

UK vs Australia. The shorts became a design project — here's where it landed and why.

The decision Locked

Two custom colourways carrying a flag badge on the right leg (SP logo lives on the left). Badges are already SP's brand language — the athlete backpack runs an English flag patch — and they're far safer to execute with Kashaf than a full custom print.

Royal Blue UK
Pantone 2965C

Swatches are screen approximations of the Pantone references, not print-accurate.

Why not a flag print

Rejected plain red/blue (won't resell), a full Union Jack print — "a Union Jack with a fat logo slapped on the side" — and flag-as-primary-print. The commercial driver: last event produced heavily-engaging fighter photos in shorts SP then couldn't sell. The Matty-in-leopard-print shot is now a converting ad. Custom event colours that double as sellable colourways solve that.

How the badge was arrived at Briefed

Square vs rounded vs circular, worked through in the room. Considered a Team GB / Team AUS emblem style (the understated circle read best — a design element, not a badge planted on), three lions, boxing kangaroo, coat of arms. Constraints applied: must be stitchable, must not disrespect the flag, must not clash with the short colour. "AU" vs "AUS" debated — AUD is currency, AUS is the sporting form. Now briefed through to Kashaf with the Pantone references.

Kashaf / Combo Sports — the relationship is the bottleneck3 fixes

Single point of contact. Ask Combo Sports to nominate one person. Current failure mode is multiple people and briefs getting lost — the Animal Series brief came back as his own interpretation.

Brief template. Build a reusable shorts-design brief format so specs land first time. This is the recurring bottleneck on every new design and the highest-leverage unlock available.

Pantone book in hand. Ash brought it over. Historically his colours come back off-spec. But his printed colours diverge from the book — 202C reads burgundy in the book, pinky-red in his prints. Verify he prints true to Pantone before trusting a custom colour.

Sampling direction. ~15 sample pairs across assorted Pantones to find 2–3 winners. Deep and luxury, not bright — "BMW leather stitching, Bugatti". Deep green, deep blue, deep red. Hermès orange floated. Also crop tops, beachy shorts, a washed-stone colour. Adam's off the pastels; Electric Blue's underperformance is his evidence SP isn't a bright brand.

Deep red unresolved. 202C too pink · 1817 browns out · 200C too boxing-glove. Parked → three sample colourways ordered instead: Deep Red 1815 PC, Alabama Burgundy 15.100.78.71, Marshall Cream.

Women's range — one fix awayBlocked

The problem. Too much material at the join between the inner bike short and the outer pant. It pulls and bunches when kicking. Sample 3 fits perfectly and is better, but the pull remains.

Root cause unresolved — material or pattern? Two diagnostics: ask Kashaf for samples in SP microfiber, and ask Michaela to pinch-test a standard unisex pair to isolate the variable.

Decision: out of Order 1, 200 units in Order 2 once fixed. Ordering it unfixed is a risk Adam won't take. Content can still proceed on the current sample — treat it as a feature piece, don't shoot close on the problem area.

Moying pack (Muay Ying — female fighter): gloves + shins + women's shorts + crop + tee ≈ $500. Works as both a viral comp prize and a sellable bundle.

Shoot direction: empowering, boxing gym, dark and moody. Not the Pursuit look — the Pursuit logos on the bags are visible and a problem. Feature to demo: foam in the pocket, go for a run.

Excalibur — gloves need V2, shins nearly thereEngaged

Shin guards — close. Weight good, feel good. The extra safety stitching addressing foot-pad detachment is a genuine win — that's the number-one failure mode on any shin pad. Fix needed: wrinkles and creases through one section, suspected gluing/application method rather than the leather; it looks unattached in places.

Velcro question. The plastic-backed velcro is unfamiliar. Is it better quality? Does it last longer? Explain why — if it's best-in-market, SP wants that in the marketing.

Gloves — need another revision. Three issues: the wrist strap and support lack stability and collapse through that section (a premium glove shows no collapse down the barrel, and the collapse causes lateral movement); padding distribution is uneven, chunky toward the forearm and thin toward the blade, when blade padding matters equally for late defence; and the shape is too flat rather than rounded, so it doesn't encapsulate the top of the hand and you get internal lateral wrist flexion however tight it's strapped.

Hand wraps must not be the fix. Gloves loosen over roughly six months, so they have to start tight.

Positives: build quality good, stitching neat. Patch much smaller — different, not worse, possibly a machine constraint.

Commercials. US$23–24 on the shin guard ≈ A$35. Bond was in the $40s on discounted bulk. Adam's framing: "I'd much rather spend $20k on shorts that gets us $100k" — Excalibur needs volume to justify itself.

Where it's at: Ameer engaged. SP sending him a glove to copy, moving into the next round of product tests. Shins then go on fighters' legs for real feedback before ordering.

Pride / Carly — the partnership ask listMeeting next week

Show is approximately October. Reframe as a two-way partnership — this is where "what does she bring to the table" belongs.

SP's ask list: event tickets · a stand or table · photos and videos as standard, and how her videographer works · social promotion · sponsor call-outs · banners (check lead time) · pay-per-view and livestream exposure (PPV is the biggest bang) · post-fight interviews · Phat Chats filmed on site · a comp giving away a Pride experience · a 3-show minimum lock-in with written terms · the show roadmap, dates and fighters.

SP pays its own flights and accommodation — that's sponsor-standard. Shorts offer to be decided next week; baseline is main card, all of them if there's spare.

Videographer economics. Flying Robbie from Perth costs roughly $600 more all-in than using Carly's local. Verdict: pay for Robbie — tried and tested, needs no directing, and delivers behind-the-scenes (airport content, "on our way to Pride, come see us at the show"). Framing: "we invest heavily in product — we need to start investing in the brand."

Promoter landscape. Empire = WA, Pride = SA, Queensland is the gap. War on the Shore used to be big, now declining. Adam's economics read: the amateur side out-earns pro, and big pro shows lose money — ~$80k purses plus sanctioning against 350 tickets at $100. "More interested in looking good than being profitable." He's pushing Darius for a balance of shows and funding sponsors before committing to big ones.

Athletes + The Fat HuntNew campaign

Roster moves. Michaela — sponsoring, decided. Ambrose — re-engage once Bond lands. Jaga Chan — contracted; call, send a care package plus 16oz for training, and ask for gym referrals. Prospects: James Honey (via Jaga's gym) and Jadeo (Married At First Sight profile play).

Punching bags rejected as a gym play — bag branding doesn't drive purchase at ~$100 a bag. Better route: kit out the coach. "We want you on our podcast, here's all the gear" → he wears it, SP gets the episode.

The Fat Hunt. "Supa Phat is on the lookout for its 2027 athlete." Entry is your best knockout or best fight footage — broadened deliberately so it isn't limited to KO artists or currently-active fighters. Prize: a 12-month SP sponsorship, all the latest gear, and access to in-product testing.

The real value is as an ad — the whole industry sees that SP has products in testing. Needs a landing page; naming treatment TBC. Matt films a talk-to-camera explainer, entrants hold a selfie and say in 30 seconds who and where. If a high-profile fighter enters, SP can still sponsor them separately off the back of it.

Black Friday & Q4 ramp27 Nov

Staged playbook: Ramp-up → Hype & Tease → Launch. Ramp-up gets every product live and advertises everything at full price. Hype and tease runs the viral comp and list generation, gives away top-tier product, and warms the range by email. Launch is a three-day countdown, then go.

The mechanic: ads buy cheap leads up front, the viral comp captures contacts permanently, email does the heavy lifting at launch, and ads scale into it.

Known winner: $50 shorts. 20% off site-wide does not work. Consequence — last year's creative shows the old offer and needs reshooting once the offer is set. The learning phase is about finding the offer.

Only one deal in November. Loy Krathong also falls in November; don't stack them. Near-term, Queen's Birthday on 12 August is a sign-up-to-win moment, though the women's shorts aren't ready and they were the natural prize.

Viral comp stock: the 10–11 spare Shadow Series pairs with the old leg badge. Launch September, run into Black Friday, with Empire QR codes feeding it. Tooling — Kickoff Labs considered, but preference is a plugin piping straight into Klaviyo. "Much easier to get email addresses at a show than sales."

Billboards — Matt wants one for SP. Awareness channel, not performance. Good for launches and specific placements.
