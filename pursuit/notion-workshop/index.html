Definition

An operational system is where the work lives in a place, not in a person.

Test 01

Can a new starter find it without asking?

The timetable template, the supplier, the brand kit. If finding it means ringing someone, it lives in a person.

Test 02

Can someone else pick it up halfway?

Sick day, holiday, resignation. The task has to carry enough that the next person keeps going.

Test 03

Does it keep working when you are on leave?

If the opening slows down because one person is away, the system is that person.

If the answer to any of the three is no, it lives in a person.
  • Read the definition twice. It is the sentence you want them repeating by the end.
  • The three tests are how they judge every card they write this afternoon. Come back to them in hands-on.

What is Notion

A shared workspace where our pages, tasks and documents live in one place, and everyone sees the same thing.

Block 01

Pages

A document that lives online. Anyone with access opens the same, current version. No attachments, no v3_FINAL.

Block 02

Databases

A list of things: tasks, clubs, SOPs. Each item has properties like owner, team, phase, status.

Block 03

Views and filters

The same list, sliced. By person, by team, by phase. As a board, a table, a timeline or a calendar.

  • Read the one-liner. Then: everything in Notion is one of these three things.
  • Page = document. Database = list. View = a way of looking at the list.
  • The key idea that trips people up: a task is a page. Open any card and it is a document you can write in, attach to, comment on.

The standard Keep this open in Part 2

Six rules of a good card

1

One task, one owner

If two people own it, split it into two cards.

2

The title says what done looks like

Start with a verb. "Book the electrician for base build", not "Electrical".

3

A new starter could act on the description

What gets made, to what standard, using what. No assumed knowledge.

4

Links live in the card

Template, brand kit, supplier, login page. Never in someone's head or inbox.

5

Team and phase are tagged

So the board filters properly and the sprint can see what's coming.

6

Optional cards say when to remove them

"Remove if…" on the card. The next board owner decides in seconds.

  • Read all six. Then tell them this slide stays on the TV for the whole of Part 2.
  • Rule 3 is the one people skip. The description is where the value is. It is also the bit only they can write.
Moving cards

Four statuses. Move the card, not the meeting.

To do

Not started. Has an owner and a phase.
→

In progress

Someone is on it this week.
→

Blocked

Waiting on someone or something. Say who, in a comment.
→

Done

Meets the description. Not "mostly".

Stuck?

Move it to Blocked and comment who you are waiting on. Don't hold it for the meeting. The person you need sees it the same day.

Doesn't apply to this club?

Mark it Not needed with a one-line reason. Don't delete it. The template learns from why it was skipped.

  • Keep statuses to four. Boards fail when someone adds "Waiting on Adam", "Almost done", "Review".
  • Blocked with a comment is the async move. It replaces the phone call and it leaves a record.
  • Not needed, not deleted. Otherwise the template loses the slab example.

The format · 20 to 30 minutes

  • Board owner opens the board. Round the room, by owner.
  • Each owner: what lands this week, what do you need, what's blocked.
  • Nobody reports what they did. The board already says that.
  • Decisions and new tasks go straight onto cards, in the meeting.

  • The difference from a normal update meeting: nobody narrates last week. You can read last week. The meeting is for what is coming and what is stuck.

Between sprints

Async by default.
We keep moving.

What async means

The work moves without waiting for a meeting or a reply

You update the card when you do the work. Whoever needs to know reads it when they need to. Nobody is on hold.

What it replaces

The phone call for an update

Status and a comment on the card do the job. Managers check the board, not the phone. Questions go on the card too, so the answer is there next time.

What you get back

Cover, handovers, and a record

Someone away? Their cards are visible. Someone new? Their onboarding is a board. Review time? The work is already written down.

Perth, Melbourne, Newcastle, Adelaide. Different time zones, one board. Async is how a national team works without everyone being in the room.
  • Define async plainly. The range of knowledge in the room is wide. Do not assume the word means anything to everyone.
  • The manager habit change is the hard one: check the board before you pick up the phone. Adam and Courtney model this or nobody does.
  • NSW and SA make this non-optional. A three-hour time difference and a phone-call culture do not mix.
