A 38-minute moderated walkthrough of homerun.app/switch with a real NSW energy customer, using his own live Red Energy bills. He reached the end of the funnel and was ready to switch — but hit one hard blocker and missed the gift card entirely on his first pass.

Why he was on Red in the first place — points, not price

Armin didn't choose Red on rates. He chose it for Qantas points: roughly 15,000–20,000 on signup plus a second tranche at around six months. Both had already landed, which is precisely why he was open to moving.

Checked live in-session, 15,000 Qantas points valued at roughly $180–$300 on flight redemption. He has real history of extracting that value — a Thailand ticket for $150 cash on points. So for this segment, points are a live competing currency to a gift card, not a soft perk. Worth knowing what Switch is bidding against.

The rates behind the saving

This is the part worth understanding, because the saving is not coming from a cheaper usage rate. Armin's first reaction on seeing the offered plan was that it looked worse than what he already had — "this is more than what I pay."

The offered usage rate is 4c/kWh higher. For the total to still come out ahead, the saving has to be carried entirely by a lower daily supply charge — meaning his Red daily charge must exceed $1.66/day. His actual figure was never read aloud, so that last step is inferred from the direction of the maths, not quoted.

Product implication

A user comparing the single most visible number — the usage rate — will conclude the offer is worse and bounce. The saving lives in the supply charge, which nobody reads. If the comparison page can't lead with total annual cost instead of unit rates, Switch needs to pre-frame that before handoff.

The 12-month cliff

The plan goes backwards after the first year. Stated in-session: the full second year would cost roughly $64 more than staying put.

That directly contradicts the $42/year ongoing saving from the same comparison output. unreconciled One of the two is wrong. Either way the operational conclusion is identical, and it's the strategic point of the whole product:

"Put a little to-do in your to-do list for in six months' time — switch again. Just rinse and repeat."

— the behaviour Switch has to build, not a one-off transaction

Armin took that on board immediately. This is the strongest argument in the session for switch reminders as a core product loop rather than a nice-to-have: a single switch leaves the user worse off in year two, so the retention mechanic is the value proposition.

06Friction log

Six issues, in order of how much they cost us. The first one ends the session for an unmoderated user.

Blocker

NRMA membership is a required field with no non-member escape

On the NRMA-branded Perks plan, the membership field is mandatory. Armin holds NRMA CTP car insurance but no membership, and the form would not let him proceed or skip: "So I don't have that. What do I do now? It doesn't let me do anything else."

Page copy elsewhere implied a membership would be created for him — directly contradicting the required field. A separate non-member "Energy Perks" variant exists at identical rates (166c/day, 33c/kWh), but he only found it after being told to go back and look. Unmoderated, this is where the session ends.

His own recommendation: signpost that a non-member path exists, at the point the membership field appears.

Action: raise with the fulfilment partner — required-field logic and the non-member alternative.
Blocker

The $150 gift card is invisible where the decision gets made

The incentive that Armin said would carry his decision on its own sits in a small bar at the top of the comparison page. He scrolled straight past it and didn't know it existed. When he later saw it on the pre-switch page his reaction was immediate — "oh, I get $150 as well… it would be a no-brainer."

It's also absent from the confirmation page, so a user who missed it never learns they're owed one.

The comparison page is partner-hosted — we can track it but not change its HTML or CSS. So the fix has to happen on our side of the handoff: carry the gift card prominently through every Homerun-controlled surface before and after, and push the partner for placement.

Action: gift card treatment on all Homerun-owned pages incl. confirmation; placement request to partner.
High

No confirmation email arrived during the session

Armin submitted and waited: "Now I'm expecting to see an email coming to me… so I haven't received it." His workaround was to screenshot the confirmation screen. Account numbers needed for provider login are expected to come from the retailer after handoff, but nothing set that expectation on screen.

At minimum the confirmation page should state what arrives, from whom, and when.

Action: verify the transactional email fires; set expectations on the confirmation screen.
High

Onboarding has no "I already live here, just comparing" option

Armin had recently moved and none of the presented situations matched him: "I don't belong to any of this… I wouldn't check any of this." His conclusion is the dangerous part — "this is probably not for me, it's designed for someone else."

That's a bounce risk on the highest-intent segment in the market: an existing customer actively shopping their current plan.

Action: add a compare-my-current-plan path to onboarding.
High

Gift card timing is stated two different ways

30 days in one reading of the terms, three to four months in another. Covered in §04 — listed here because it's a live copy defect, not just an open question.

Action: confirm the real fulfilment window; correct the terms.
Medium

NRMA partnership benefit isn't explained where it's asked for

Armin couldn't tell what the NRMA relationship gave him — he guessed roadside assistance. The explanation exists further up the page (complimentary membership, travel and experience deals) but not near the field that demands it. He read it as another points scheme.

Action: surface the benefit inline at the membership field.

07What worked

  • The form itself. "Very straightforward" — confirmed twice, unprompted. No friction reported in the flow mechanics, only in one field's logic.
  • The design. "I really like how easy and simple the design is."
  • Positioning landed with zero explanation. Mid-session, unprompted: "I do understand Home Run is just a platform that connects you to the providers." No confusion about what Switch is or who supplies the energy.
  • The saving was real and he believed it. "Okay, I can save some money" — followed immediately by going back to select a plan. The comparison did its job.
  • Incentive stacking worked on him. Once the gift card was visible, price stopped mattering. That's a lever, provided the card is actually visible.

08What this changes

Fix before the next cohort of traffic

  • NRMA required-field dead end, and the non-member path signposting.
  • Gift card visibility across every Homerun-controlled surface, including confirmation.
  • One consistent answer on gift card timing.
  • Resolve the $392-vs-$592 double-count before any figure is quoted internally or externally.

Design decisions this surfaces

  • Lead with total annual cost, not unit rates. Armin's instinct was to compare the usage rate and conclude the offer was worse. The saving hides in the supply charge.
  • Switch reminders are the product, not a feature. A one-off switch leaves the user behind in year two. The repeat loop is where the value actually is.
  • Two completely different user methods need supporting. Armin exported the page to PDF and had an AI do the whole comparison. A separate older participant interviewed previously itemised everything into a spreadsheet by hand. Same job, opposite tooling.
  • Points are the incumbent competitor. Armin stayed with Red for Qantas points and only became switchable once both tranches had landed. A gift card is competing against a loyalty currency users overvalue — and there's a timing signal in there worth targeting.
One correction for the record

The owned survey stat was quoted in this session as 67%. The claims bank figure is 69% of Australian sharehouses not having compared their energy plan in 12 months, cited to the 2026 survey of 3,672 Australian sharehousers. 69% is the substantiated number and the only one approved for use.
