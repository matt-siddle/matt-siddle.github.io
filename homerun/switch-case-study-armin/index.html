He was on Red for points, not price

15,000–20,000 Qantas points on signup plus a second tranche at ~6 months. Both had landed — which is exactly why he was switchable. Checked live: 15,000 points ≈ $180–$300 on flight redemption, and he has form extracting it (Thailand ticket, $150 cash on points).

So the gift card is bidding against a loyalty currency users overvalue — and there's a timing signal worth targeting.

The saving is in the supply charge, not the rate

The offered usage rate is 4c/kWh higher. Armin's first reaction was that the offer looked worse — "this is more than what I pay." The saving is carried entirely by a lower daily supply charge, which nobody reads. (His Red daily charge must exceed $1.66/day; never read aloud, so that step is inferred.)

A user comparing the most visible number concludes the offer is worse and bounces. Lead with total annual cost, or pre-frame it before handoff.

A single switch leaves the user worse off in year two. So the reminder loop isn't a feature — it is the value proposition.

07What worked

  • The form. "Very straightforward" — confirmed twice, unprompted. Friction was in one field's logic, not the flow.
  • The design. "I really like how easy and simple the design is."
  • Positioning landed with zero explanation. Unprompted, mid-session: "I do understand Home Run is just a platform that connects you to the providers."
  • He believed the saving. "Okay, I can save some money" — then went straight back to select a plan.
  • Incentive stacking worked. Once the gift card was visible, price stopped mattering.

08What this changes

Fix before the next cohort of traffic

  • NRMA required-field dead end + non-member signposting.
  • Gift card visibility on every Homerun-controlled surface, confirmation included.
  • One answer on gift card timing.
  • Resolve $392 vs $592 before any figure gets quoted.

Design decisions this surfaces

  • Lead with total annual cost, not unit rates. The saving hides in the supply charge.
  • Switch reminders are the product. One switch leaves the user behind in year two.
  • Two user methods to support. Armin let an AI do the whole comparison. A previous older participant itemised it into a spreadsheet by hand. Same job, opposite tooling.
  • Points are the incumbent competitor, and the moment both bonus tranches land is the switchable window.
One correction for the record

The owned survey stat was quoted in-session as 67%. The claims bank figure is 69% of Australian sharehouses not having compared their energy plan in 12 months, cited to the 2026 survey of 3,672 Australian sharehousers. 69% is the substantiated number and the only one approved for use.
